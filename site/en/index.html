What makes it different

Events stay in Google Calendar. These are the things RingCal adds.

  • Per-event alarms that keep ringing

    Turn the alarm on for single events and choose how early it rings, from on time to 24 hours ahead. It rings full screen, even when the phone is locked or silent, until you dismiss it.

  • ⏰ syncs with your computer

    Turn an alarm on in the app and ⏰ is added to the title in Google Calendar. Add ⏰ to a title on your computer and the phone rings too.

  • You decide when the day starts

    Set “Day starts at” in Settings (06:00 by default). The day and N-day timelines start there, so you don't scroll past the night every time; earlier events still show. All-day event alarms ring at this time too.

  • Custom days and weeks

    More than day, week and month: see 2 to 7 days on the timeline, or only 1 to 6 weeks in the grid with bigger cells. The home screen month widget can do the same.

  • Alarm list, skip once

    One page lists every alarm in the next 7 days. Skip a single occurrence when you don't need it; the rest ring as usual.

  • No false alarms

    Before ringing, it checks that the event still exists, the time has not changed and you have not declined it. Change or delete the event on a computer and the alarm follows. If alarm volume is 0 or a permission is off, the home screen tells you.

  • Snooze without missing it

    Snooze for 5, 10 or 15 minutes. Alarms at the same time share one screen. If nobody responds it stops and leaves a “Missed alarm” notification; alarms missed while the phone was off ring after startup if they were within 30 minutes.

  • Add events by voice

    Tap the microphone and say “Meeting with the client tomorrow at 3 pm” to add it; say “with an alarm” to turn the alarm on. There is also a 1×1 home screen widget with only a microphone.

  • Your colors, dark included

    Settings › Appearance changes the accent and background to any color; a dark background gives a dark layout, widgets included. Colors that are too light or dark are adjusted so text stays readable. Calendars can also be recolored in the app without touching Google Calendar.

Everything a calendar needs

Viewing and adding events works much like Google Calendar, so there is nothing to relearn.

  • Full-page month

    Each cell shows that day's events (and lunar dates if you want them); multi-day events span cells. Swipe to change month.

  • Big, readable text

    The day view uses large text and generous spacing, readable even with the system font at 1.5×. Overlapping events sit side by side; tap an empty slot to add one.

  • Search

    Find events by title, location or notes within a year of today. Results are sorted by date and open at the first one from today on.

  • Add and edit like Google

    The editor follows Google Calendar's layout: Google's 11 event colors, locations you used before, and up to 5 notifications.

  • Home screen widgets

    The month widget shows the whole month or your custom days or weeks, with multi-day events as one bar. There is also an agenda widget and the 1×1 voice widget.

  • Handles “Add to calendar”

    When another app adds to the calendar or opens an event or date, you can choose RingCal, once or always.

Safe to use

No network, no data collection, source in the open.

  • No network permission

    The app cannot go online at all. Events are read and written through the phone's calendar database, and the Google account on your phone syncs them, exactly as with Google Calendar. Voice add uses the phone's built-in voice input; the app only receives the text.

  • No sign-up, no data collected

    No account, no ads, no analytics. Settings and alarm states stay on your phone.

  • Open source

    The code is public on GitHub under the MIT License. Anyone can inspect, change and build it.

  • Never changes titles others see

    ⏰ is added to the title only for events you created with no other guests. For meetings others invited you to, the alarm is kept on your phone only.

Questions

Why is it not on Google Play?

RingCal is a free app made by one person. Publishing needs a developer account and extra declarations for alarm and full-screen permissions; installing the APK is simpler. All the code is public, so you can check it.

I get an “unknown source” or security warning when installing.

That is normal for apps installed outside Google Play: Android asks you to confirm the source. Make sure the file came from this site, then allow the install.

I installed 1.0.x. Why can't I update directly?

From 1.1.0 the package name and signature changed (the developer name is now C520), so Android treats it as a different app. Remove the old version and install the new one; with both installed, events with an alarm ring twice.

Your events are in Google Calendar and are not affected; alarms marked ⏰ in the title keep working. Settings on the phone, alarms kept only on the phone and home screen widgets need setting up again. Later versions update in place.

Are my events uploaded anywhere?

No. RingCal has no network permission and no server. Your events already live in your Google Calendar, and the Google account on your phone does the syncing.

How does voice add work? Where does my voice go?

Tap the microphone on the widget or at the bottom right of the app and say the date, time and event, for example “Security meeting on December 1 from 9 am to 12 pm”. It is added to the default calendar chosen in Settings, with a notification you can tap to edit or delete it. Say “with an alarm” or “wake me” to turn the alarm on. If the date, time or title is missing, or the event repeats, the editor opens so you can finish it.

Recording and recognition are handled by the phone's built-in Google voice input, the same as the microphone on your keyboard, and may be sent to Google's speech service. RingCal itself has no network permission and only receives the text.

How soon do events I add or change show up in Google Calendar?

On the phone, right away. Uploading to Google is done by Android's sync, usually within seconds to a few minutes; longer when the phone is hot, offline or saving power. To sync now, open Google Calendar and pull down to refresh.

What is the ⏰ at the start of a title?

It marks an alarm. Turn the alarm on in RingCal and “⏰ ” is added to the title in Google Calendar; turn it off and it is removed. The other way round, add ⏰ to a title on your computer and the phone rings.

Titles of meetings with other guests are never changed; the alarm is kept on your phone only.

Can I use it together with the Google Calendar app?

Yes. Both show the same events. Alarms ring only on phones with RingCal installed.

Do alarms still ring after the phone restarts?

Yes. They are scheduled again after startup. Alarms missed while the phone was off ring if they were within 30 minutes; older ones leave a “Missed alarm” notification. After a restart, unlock the phone once so the app can read the calendar.

Why does it suggest “Unrestricted” battery usage?

It is optional. Alarms use the system's exact alarms and ring on time even in battery saver. Unrestricted battery usage only makes changes from your computer reach the phone's alarms sooner.

Does it work on iPhone?

No. It needs a phone with Android 13 or later.

Can I change the source or share it?

Yes. RingCal is released under the MIT License : you may use, change and share it freely as long as you keep the copyright and license notice. Licenses of the open source components are in the app under Settings › About › Open source licenses.

Is RingCal related to Google?

No. RingCal is an independent project by one person and is not endorsed by Google. Google, Google Calendar and Android are trademarks of Google LLC.
